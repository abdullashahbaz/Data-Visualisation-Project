## Getting Started

Open `index.html` using **Live Server** in VS Code (right-click → Open with Live Server),
or run a local server from the project folder:

```bash
python3 -m http.server 8080
# Then open: http://localhost:8080
```

> **Note:** An internet connection is needed for the world map to load. The map base
> layer is fetched from a public source at startup.

## The Controls Bar

| Control | What it does |
|---------|-------------|
| **Find Country** | Select a specific country. Updates all four charts at once. |
| **Min Total Medals** | Hides countries below the medal threshold from the bubble chart. |
| **Min GDP/Capita** | Hides countries below the GDP threshold from the bubble chart. |
| **✕ Clear Selection** | Removes the current selection and returns all charts to the full view. |

> The medal and GDP filters only affect the bubble chart.
> The map, bump chart, and timeline always show the full dataset.

## Chapter 1 – Bubble Chart (GDP vs Total Medals)

### What you see
Each circle is a country. Its **left/right position** is the country's average GDP per
capita from 1960 to 2016, on a log scale. Its **up/down position** is the total number
of medals won. The **size** of the circle shows how many athletes that country sent to
the Olympics (counted per event entry, as generated by the group's EDA notebook).

Below the chart you will see three reference circles showing what ~500, ~2,000, and
~5,000 athlete entries look like at scale.

### How to interact

| Action | What happens |
|--------|-------------|
| **Hover** a bubble | Tooltip: country, GDP/capita, total medals, athlete count |
| **Click** a bubble | Selects that country — highlights it, dims all others, and updates the map, bump chart, and timeline |
| **Click** the same bubble again | Deselects (returns to full view) |
| **Scroll** on the chart | Zooms in on the scatter plot |
| **Drag** after zooming | Pans around the zoomed area |

### What to look for
- **Top right** (high GDP, many medals): USA, Germany, Australia — wealthy nations with
  large Olympic programmes
- **Top left outliers** (low GDP, many medals): Cuba, Kenya, Jamaica — countries that
  win far more than their wealth would suggest
- **Bottom right** (high GDP, very few medals): small wealthy nations like Luxembourg
  or Qatar

## Chapter 1 – Medal Efficiency Map

### What you see
Countries are coloured by how many medals they win per $10,000 of GDP per capita.
Darker red/orange means the country is more efficient — winning more medals relative
to its wealth. Countries with no matching data appear in dark grey.

### How to interact

| Action | What happens |
|--------|-------------|
| **Hover** a country | Tooltip: efficiency score, total medals, GDP/capita |
| **Click** a country | Same as clicking its bubble — selects it across all charts |
| **Scroll** | Zooms into any region |
| **Drag** | Pans the map |

### What to look for
- **Eastern Europe** (Hungary, Romania, Cuba) shows very high efficiency
- **East Africa / Caribbean** (Kenya, Jamaica) appears notably dark despite low GDP
- **Western nations** have moderate efficiency — high medals but also very high GDP

## Chapter 1 – Medals & GDP Over Time (Timeline)

This chart sits directly below the bubble and map charts and updates whenever
you select a country from either of them.

### What you see
Two lines for the selected country across each Olympic edition it participated in:
- **Yellow solid line** (left axis) – total medals won that year
- **Blue dashed line** (right axis) – GDP per capita in USD that year

Both axes are scaled to that country's own data range, so you can compare the
**shape** of each series rather than absolute values.

### How to interact

| Action | What happens |
|--------|-------------|
| **Select a country** (bubble, map, or dropdown) | Chart renders with animation |
| **Hover** a yellow dot | Year, total medals, gold/silver/bronze breakdown, GDP |
| **Hover** a blue dot | Year and GDP per capita |
| **✕ Clear Selection** | Returns chart to placeholder state |

### Suggested countries to explore
- **USA** – Does medal performance follow economic growth, or stay consistently high?
- **China** – Watch both lines climb together after the 1990s economic reforms
- **Russia** – GDP collapses post-1991, medals also fall — both move in the same direction
- **Kenya** – Low but steadily rising GDP; medals remain consistent — culture drives success
- **Australia** – Medals spike sharply around the 2000 Sydney home Games, then decline

## Chapter 2 – Bump Chart (Medal Table Rank Over Time)

This is the most information-dense chart in the application. Read this section
before interacting with it.

### What you see
Each **coloured line** represents one of the 15 highest medal-winning nations.
The chart covers **15 Summer Olympics** from 1960 to 2016.

- **x-axis** – Olympic year
- **y-axis** – Medal table rank (rank 1 = most medals, shown at the top)
- **Circle size** at each point – how many medals that country won that year
- **Gaps** in a line – the country fell outside the top 15 that year

Two **dashed vertical lines** mark politically significant editions:
- **1980 (Moscow)** – USA and allies boycotted
- **1984 (Los Angeles)** – Soviet Union and allies boycotted

### How to interact

| Action | What happens |
|--------|-------------|
| **Hover** a circle | Year, rank, total medals, gold/silver/bronze |
| **Click** a line or country label | Selects that country — highlights its line in yellow and dims others, also updates bubble chart and map |
| **Click** again | Deselects |

### What to look for
- **USA vs Soviet Union / Russia** swapping top positions throughout the Cold War
- Both nations **disappearing** at their respective boycott years (1980 and 1984)
- **China** barely visible in the early editions, then climbing rapidly from 1992 onward
  to reach rank 1 at the 2008 Beijing home Games
- **Post-1991 drop** for Russia as Soviet medals scattered across newly independent states
- **Germany** consistently high in the 1960s–80s when East and West Germany competed
  separately (combined here under Germany)

## Tips

1. Start with the bubble chart to find an interesting country — look for outliers
   far above or below the general trend line
2. Click that country — the timeline immediately appears below showing its history
3. Then scroll down to the bump chart to see whether its aggregate position
   matches what the rank-over-time line shows
4. Use the **Find Country** dropdown at the top to quickly jump between nations
5. Use **✕ Clear Selection** any time to reset all charts to the full global view
